# Services Storybook — Research Handoff

## Executive recommendation

Proceed with:

- **Copy:** Direction 1 — Direct and trustworthy
- **Visual:** The Working Field Guide
- **Short service labels:** Websites & Systems / Brand Identity / Ongoing Design
- **Full service titles:** Website Design & Digital Systems / Brand Identity & Digital Refresh / Ongoing Design Support
- **Interaction:** one opening, one prologue, three service spreads, one final CTA spread
- **Motif:** one burnt-orange line that changes function across chapters

This direction preserves commercial clarity while allowing the book object and imagery to provide character.

## Why this is the recommended direction

The current Services experience requires visitors to move through four separate beats per service. The brief instead calls for all four beats to be visible within one chapter spread, with a direct path for visitors who already know what they need.

The recommended direction:

- keeps the service titles explicit,
- avoids unsupported outcome claims,
- makes the full twelve-beat journey optional rather than compulsory,
- uses one coherent object across desktop and mobile,
- leaves final copy as accessible live text,
- avoids generic technology and agency imagery,
- gives CodeByLeon a repeatable visual motif,
- and reduces the number of major animated states.

## Recommended visual direction

### Object

A modern, tactile field guide:

- dark brown-black leather,
- quiet wear and softened edges,
- warm uncoated paper,
- stitched binding,
- realistic page block,
- restrained metal detail,
- attached chapter tabs,
- and a burnt-orange thread or drawn line.

It should feel like a working studio object rather than an antique or fantasy book.

### Chapter imagery

1. **Websites & Systems — Signal to Structure**  
   Fragmented signals align into an architectural route. The orange line becomes a user path.

2. **Brand Identity — Reframe and Reveal**  
   One established form is viewed through crop frames and translucent layers. The orange line becomes a measuring thread.

3. **Ongoing Design — Continuous Rhythm**  
   Different creative outputs move through one coherent sequence. The orange line becomes a stitch or production path.

4. **Final spread — Convergence**  
   The three line behaviours meet at one small starting point.

### Chapter tints

- Websites & Systems: cream + restrained peach
- Brand Identity: cream + muted lilac or dusty rose
- Ongoing Design: cream + subdued sage or blue-green

The tint should be felt but not treated as a large coloured panel.

## Desktop behaviour

1. The closed cover appears as the Services section entry.
2. The first valid scroll opens the cover once.
3. The prologue exposes all three chapter choices.
4. A chapter tab can open any service directly.
5. Continued scrolling turns between only the prologue, three chapters, and final spread.
6. Each chapter spread keeps all four beats visible.
7. Optional beat focus highlights one detail in the chapter illustration without hiding other beat copy.
8. The final spread remains open.
9. Fast scroll input advances at most one major state.
10. Reduced motion uses direct state changes or restrained fades.

## Mobile behaviour

1. The object becomes a portrait top-bound field notebook.
2. The cover lifts from the bottom edge toward the top hinge.
3. Pages turn bottom-to-top.
4. Chapter imagery appears above the words.
5. All four beats remain in one vertical chapter page.
6. A compact chapter selector plus explicit previous/next controls remains visible.
7. Swipe and scroll may enhance the interaction but do not replace controls.
8. Viewport changes preserve the active chapter.

## Direct and full-book routes

### Direct route

Closed cover → prologue → selected service → final CTA.

### Full route

Closed cover → prologue → Websites & Systems → Brand Identity → Ongoing Design → final CTA.

Both routes are first-class. The visitor does not choose a formal “mode.”

## Rejected alternatives

### Twelve separate page turns

Rejected because it recreates the current compulsory journey and makes comparison expensive.

### Ambiguous chapter titles such as Launch, Transform, and Scale

Rejected because they hide the actual service and introduce outcome-heavy language.

### Premium antique ledger

Rejected because ornamental detail could compete with copy and drift too close to familiar storybook references.

### Bright collage scrapbook

Rejected because it weakens the subtle chapter-tint system and risks visual noise.

### Generic laptop or dashboard scenes

Rejected because they repeat the service literally, date quickly, and do not express the deeper business problem.

### Full-image replacement for every beat

Rejected because it creates twelve hidden visual states, increases asset cost, and undermines the one-spread-per-service architecture.

### Compulsory closing animation

Rejected because it delays the project action.

## Implementation warning for Codex

Do not begin production implementation until the commercial confirmation flags above are resolved or intentionally left as cautious copy.

When implementation begins:

- work in a separate production branch,
- do not modify the research evidence,
- map the new component to one owned stylesheet,
- use `useGSAP` and ScrollTrigger for scroll-driven motion,
- maintain keyboard and reduced-motion paths,
- preserve active chapter across resize,
- prevent multiple state advances from one high-velocity input burst,
- keep all service information live and selectable,
- add image fallbacks,
- and validate both the direct and full-book routes.

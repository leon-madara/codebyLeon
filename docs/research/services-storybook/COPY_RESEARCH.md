# Services Storybook — Audience and Language Research

## Purpose and method

This document studies how Kenyan small and medium-sized businesses describe digital presence, credibility, customer contact, branding, and recurring creative work. It is directional research for service copy, not a claim that every Kenyan business uses the same language.

Evidence is separated into:

- **Observed audience or market language** — wording found in Kenyan public sources, business programmes, interviews, product descriptions, and current CodeByLeon material.
- **Informed interpretation** — a copy or product implication supported by several observations.
- **Unsupported hypotheses** — plausible ideas that should not be written as fact without client interviews, analytics, or commercial confirmation.

## Research boundaries

The available current evidence is stronger on digital adoption, customer channels, and operational barriers than on exact phrases used by buyers while procuring web-design services. Public sources also over-represent formal, connected, and urban businesses. For this reason, the recommended copy uses plain language and avoids pretending that the research proves a single national buyer vocabulary.

## Observed audience and market language

### 1. Professional websites and digital systems

Observed phrases and concepts include:

- “reach customers”
- “market access”
- “run [the business] more efficiently”
- “customer journey”
- “simple” and “easy”
- “website management”
- “online services”
- “customer needs”
- “business app”
- “productivity tools”
- “one stop shop”
- “contact,” “order,” “book,” “pay,” and “follow up”

These phrases are practical. They describe what the business and customer should be able to do, not the novelty of the technology.

### 2. Brand identity and outdated presentation

Direct Kenyan research on procurement language for brand-refresh work is limited. Across business-facing sources and current CodeByLeon copy, the recurring concerns are:

- looking professional,
- being trusted,
- presenting the business clearly,
- keeping information and visuals consistent,
- showing what the business has become,
- avoiding a dated or incomplete public image.

“Brand identity” is understood better when immediately followed by concrete examples such as logo, colours, messaging, website, social templates, and agreed touchpoints.

### 3. Recurring design and website support

Observed operational language is task-based:

- updates,
- requests,
- campaigns,
- website changes,
- marketing materials,
- support,
- review,
- delivery,
- ongoing relationship,
- avoiding multiple vendors,
- reducing complexity,
- keeping work moving.

The strongest framing is not “unlimited design” or “speed.” It is continuity: the provider already understands the business, requests follow a known process, and the team does not restart every time.

### 4. Credibility, inquiries, growth, and presentation

Observed language tends to connect digital presence with:

- being easier to find,
- being easier to understand,
- being easier to contact,
- reaching a wider customer base,
- showing prices, services, styles, or availability,
- appearing professional and current,
- making the customer journey simpler.

“Growth” is common in institutional and provider language, but it is too broad to use as a guaranteed service result. “More inquiries” is commercially relevant but remains an outcome influenced by traffic, offer quality, pricing, sales follow-up, and market conditions.

## Informed interpretation

1. **Lead with the job, then the craft.** “Website Design & Digital Systems” is clearer than “Launch,” while “Websites & Systems” remains suitable for a short tab.

2. **Use mobile-contact language without reducing the service to WhatsApp.** A website can make it easier to call, message, request a quote, book, pay, or begin a conversation. The exact integrations belong under `[CONFIRM]`.

3. **Translate agency terms.** “Information architecture” becomes “how the pages, message, and actions are organised.” “Brand system” becomes “the visual and messaging rules used across agreed touchpoints.”

4. **Frame credibility as clarity and consistency, not a guaranteed perception.** Prefer “present the business more clearly and consistently” over “make customers trust you.”

5. **Describe support as an operating model.** Request, priority, review, revision, and delivery are understandable stages. Do not promise turnaround speed without a defined service level.

6. **Keep the service titles direct.** Storytelling should sit in the image, beat title, and body copy. The chapter label must tell a visitor what is sold.

7. **Make the CTA diagnostic rather than triumphant.** “Start Your Project” or “Discuss Your Next Step” fits visitors at different stages without promising results.

## Unsupported hypotheses to test

The following may be true for some CodeByLeon prospects but are not established by the sources:

- Most prospects rely only on Instagram or WhatsApp and have no website.
- A dated identity is directly causing lost sales.
- Buyers prefer the term “digital systems” to “web apps” or “business tools.”
- Ongoing support is mainly purchased because hiring a full-time designer is unaffordable.
- A storybook metaphor will increase engagement or conversion.
- Visitors will understand “Brand Identity” without examples.
- “Ongoing Design” is clearer than “Design Support” for all audiences.
- Kenyan buyers prefer English-only service copy over light, carefully tested Swahili phrasing.
- Buyers expect WhatsApp integration by default.

These should be tested through prospect interviews, inquiry forms, sales-call notes, or lightweight label testing.

## Claim audit of the existing twelve beats

- “pixel-perfect execution” — remove; unverifiable and prohibited by the brief.
- “ready to attract, convert, and delight” — remove; implies outcomes.
- “silently losing you credibility” — soften to “may no longer present the business clearly.”
- “positions you for growth” — remove or treat as an aspiration, not a result.
- “3–4 weeks” — `[CONFIRM: whether this is a consistent brand-refresh timeline and what scope it covers]`.
- “positions you as the leader” — remove.
- “10–20 hours each month” — `[CONFIRM: whether this is the current support model and whether hours vary by plan]`.
- “fast turnarounds” — `[CONFIRM: response and delivery expectations by request type]`.
- “scalable growth” — remove as a promised result.

## Research conclusion

The safest and strongest language territory is practical, current, and commercially useful:

> Make the business easier to understand, easier to contact, and easier to keep consistent.

This is not a performance guarantee. It is a clear description of the design job and the intended customer experience.
